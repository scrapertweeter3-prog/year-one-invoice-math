One year of a hire buys almost four years of the plan

The hiring math starts before day one. A senior engineer runs $18,290 a month fully loaded, $219,480 across a year, and the same upfront fee that shows in the benefits and payroll breakdown, about $13,500, lands before a single commit exists. Add them and year one of a hire costs $232,980, and every part of that number is committed: salary, benefits at 35 percent, payroll taxes at 15, bonus at 15, retirement at 8, the equipment, the seat.

Turn the same number around and read it as subscription budget. The yearly plan prices at $4,995 a month, $59,940 over a year, and $232,980 divided by that is 3.9, so the first year of one hire covers almost four full years of the plan. Even against the monthly plan at $71,940 a year, the same $232,980 stretches to 3.2 years. The comparison is not retention or ramp time; it is one invoice for an employee against four years of capacity on demand.

Poket Dev prices that capacity at $4,995 a month on the yearly plan: one active request on a Trello board at a time, onboarding in about a day, each card shipped inside 72 hours, and revisions that never add a line to an invoice. No benefits load, no payroll taxes, no $13,500 fee before day one, and canceling returns nothing to unwind because the private repository was always yours. The same budget that keeps a hire for twelve months keeps the subscription running past year three, and the budget never becomes headcount, it becomes shipped software.
